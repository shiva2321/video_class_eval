**What it does.** The app grades an online class recording Poor, Average, Good or Excellent and explains the grade with evidence. It runs entirely on one local PC: children's recordings may not go to unapproved services, so no cloud model is used.

**Approach: measure the facts, constrain the judgement.** ffmpeg extracts audio and frames; faster-whisper transcribes; voice embeddings separate the tutor from the students; computer vision reads the screen (board, lesson video, 8x8 background) and a local vision model checks whether the tutor's face is visible while they speak; audio analysis measures talk time, pauses and clarity. A local language model reads the transcript in eight-minute chunks and fills a fixed checklist, with a reason and a verbatim tutor quote per verdict, each verified against the transcript. A transparent weighted rubric, built from the tutors' review notes, turns facts and verdicts into a score; unmeasurable items are "not assessed", never guessed. The report explains the grade in words, written from the report's own facts (sentences with unsupported numbers or names are removed), beside a table of points lost per dimension.

**How I used AI.** Claude planned and reviewed; Gemini in Antigravity implemented under written data-handling rules. I reviewed every diff and rejected failures such as a path-traversal bug and a fallback that graded a weak class Excellent.

**What I trust, and what I don't.** On eight labelled classes the exact grade matched 0.5 of the time, within one grade 0.875, rank correlation 0.5893. These are optimistic: boundaries were tuned on the same classes, and fitted leave-one-out the exact match is 0.25, chance level. Background, tutor visibility, start time and question timing proved useful; audio and warmth measures missed flagged problems. Transcript checks are English-only; other languages get measured signals and a capped grade.
