**What it does.** The app grades an online class recording Poor, Average, Good or Excellent and explains the grade with evidence. It runs entirely on one local PC: children's recordings may not go to unapproved services, so no cloud model is used.

**Approach: measure the facts, constrain the judgement.** ffmpeg extracts audio and frames; faster-whisper transcribes; voice embeddings separate the tutor from the students; computer vision reads the screen (board, lesson video, tutor tile, 8x8 background, camera-off card); audio analysis measures talk time, pauses and clarity. A local language model reads the transcript in eight-minute chunks and fills a fixed checklist, with a one-sentence reason and a verbatim tutor quote per verdict. Every quote is verified against the transcript and names are masked. A transparent weighted rubric, built from the tutors' review notes, turns facts and verdicts into a score; anything that cannot be measured is shown as "not assessed", never guessed. The report also shows what the class covered, who spoke, and how questions were asked, answered and followed up.

**How I used AI.** Claude planned and reviewed; Gemini in Antigravity implemented under written data-handling rules. I read every diff, reran tests, and rejected failures: a path-traversal bug, a test backdoor, and a fallback that graded a weak class Excellent.

**What I trust, and what I don't.** On eight labelled classes the exact grade matched 0.25 of the time, within one grade 0.875, rank correlation 0.5107. These are optimistic: settings were tuned on the same classes. The 8x8 background check, how soon the tutor starts, and question timing were useful; camera, audio and warmth measures missed problems the reviewers flagged. Transcript checks are English-only; other languages get measured signals and a capped grade. The tutor's voice sometimes splits in two, understating their talk time.
